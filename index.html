Typical situationsWhat business owners call about

Restroom out of service

A toilet that overflows or will not flush, a urinal that runs nonstop, or a floor drain backing up. With a single restroom, this is urgent.

Break-room sink and disposal

Coffee grounds and food scraps slow the drain, and the disposal under it starts to leak or jam.

Water found after closing

A cleaning crew or alarm company reports water on the floor overnight. Someone has to find the source fast.

Water heater trouble

No hot water for handwashing, a tank dripping in a utility closet, or a unit that cannot keep up with demand.

Low pressure or no water

Fixtures barely flow, or the whole suite loses water, sometimes after nearby utility work.

Sewer smell or backup

Odors near floor drains or several fixtures backing up together, which points to the building's main line.

Right nowIf water is on the floor at your business

  1. Stop the source. Close the fixture valve behind the toilet or under the sink. If you cannot, find the building or suite main shutoff.
  2. Protect people. Put out a wet-floor sign or close the area. Keep staff and customers away from water near outlets or equipment.
  3. Protect stock and records. Lift boxes, paper files, and electronics off the floor.
  4. Call (229) 367-1193. Describe the problem and whether the business is open or closed.
  5. Tell your landlord or property manager if you lease, especially when the leak may involve shared pipes.
Tip: tape a note inside the janitor closet showing where the main shutoff is and who to call. The person who finds the leak is often not the person who knows the building.

Food serviceStaff kitchens and small food businesses

Cafés, bakeries, and offices with busy break rooms put far more through their drains than a household kitchen does. Starches, grease, and coffee grounds collect in the horizontal pipe behind the wall, and the sink that drained fine on Monday is standing full by Friday. Routine drain cleaning for these kitchens keeps the line open, and jetting can clear heavier buildup that a cable leaves behind.

Signs your kitchen line needs attention

  • Sinks that drain slower during the lunch rush and recover overnight.
  • A sour smell from floor drains near prep areas.
  • Gurgling from one sink when another is emptied.
  • A disposal that hums but does not turn, or drips from underneath.

PreventionA simple plumbing maintenance routine

Most businesses do not need a complex program. A short monthly walk-through catches small leaks early and keeps surprises down.

  • Flush every toilet and run every faucet, listening for running water afterward.
  • Look under restroom and break-room sinks for damp cabinet floors or drips.
  • Glance at the floor around the water heater for drips or corrosion.
  • Read the water meter at closing and again at opening; any change means water moved overnight.
  • Post a sign asking staff not to pour grease or coffee grounds down the sink.
  • Keep floor drains clear of boxes and mop buckets.

FAQQuestions from Albany business owners

Can a plumber come after we close?

Yes. The dispatch line operates around the clock, so you can ask for work outside business hours to avoid disrupting customers. Tell the dispatcher your preferred window and how the plumber will get in.

We lease our space. Should we call or the landlord?

Check your lease, but in an emergency, stopping the water comes first. Many tenants call and let the landlord know at the same time. Share the property manager's contact so approvals are not delayed.

Our only restroom is out of order. Is that an emergency?

For most businesses it is, since staff and customers need access. Mention that it is the only restroom when you call.

Water appeared overnight and nobody knows where it came from. What now?

Shut off the main if water is still spreading, take photos, and call. A plumber can trace the source, whether it is a fixture, a supply line, the water heater, or a drain.

Do you handle both homes and businesses?

Yes. The same phone line serves homes and businesses across Albany, Georgia, so business owners can use it for their own houses too.
